SST:PV Perseveration Model

Does the empirically-grounded shift in SST:PV inhibitory ratio across development explain the age-related decline in behavioral perseveration?

At a glance

Question: a rate-model decision circuit is given only the real, isolated SST/PV ChR2 output-synapse fold-changes across age. Is that alone sufficient to produce higher behavioral perseveration (sticky Q-learning κ) in the young parameterization than the old one — with no stickiness term written into the circuit anywhere?

Answer: yes, directionally, and now on reasonably firm statistical ground. Combined across 12 independent real-sized (16/group) simulated cohorts, young > old κ reaches significance overall (Fisher's combined p = 0.019) and is not fragile to any of the circuit's named free parameters (time constants, coupling strength).

One claim retracted: an earlier single-run report claimed the model also reproduced the real data's late-session-specific kappa decline. That does not replicate — see Replication study. Treat this as a sufficiency demonstration for the overall direction, not an explanation of the late-session timing.

Background

Isolated, cell-type-specific ChR2 slice-electrophysiology recordings show two age-related synaptic changes: SST→pyramidal output strength declines with age, while PV→pyramidal output strength increases. Feedforward IPSC kinetics in the compound (non-isolated) signal shift toward PV-like speed with age — a composite measurement kept deliberately out of Phase 1/2's parameterization, since it conflates several separate quantities (see References).

Separately, sticky Q-learning fits (Rescorla-Wagner value updates + a choice-stickiness parameter κ) to the real 80-20 bandit cohort show male mice becoming reliably less perseverative with age, an effect specific to late sessions (6-10 of 10) that survives correction for multiple comparisons.

Phase 1 asks whether a circuit built only from the first fact is sufficient to produce the qualitative direction of the second, with perseveration emerging from network dynamics rather than being fit or coded in.

Model construction

A Wilson-Cowan-style rate circuit: two excitatory pools (E0, E1) accumulate value/evidence for the bandit's two options. Each pool recruits its own local PV and SST interneuron drive, which inhibits the competing pool — standard lateral/cross-inhibition wiring for winner-take-all decision circuits.

PV
fast · divisive gain on the competing pool's output
SST
slow · subtractive shift of the competing pool's input

Neither the excitatory pools nor the interneuron populations are reset between trials or sessions—session boundaries excepted, where state resets along with the internal value estimate, matching real sessions being separated by rest days. Whatever choice-repetition bias emerges is a side effect of how completely PV (fast) vs. SST (slow) inhibition clears residual pool activity during the inter-trial interval, regression-tested directly (see Mechanism check) rather than assumed.

The model has no direct PV↔SST synaptic connection in either direction — sidestepping a real, asymmetric connectivity question (SST inhibits PV; the reverse is comparatively weak) that doesn't need resolving for this test. Full literature grounding for every design choice is in References.

Parameterization

SST and PV synaptic weights are set from real ChR2 dose-response curves (evoked charge, fC/pF, vs. laser power), read at the saturating (~15 mW) plateau of each age group's curve — graph-read estimates, not exact per-cell values.

Cell typeYoung (6-9wk)Old (16-36wk)Fold-change (old/young)

Interneuron kinetics (PV τ=15 ms, SST τ=100 ms) are literature-typical values, held fixed across age — only the output weights above scale with age, so any age-shift in emergent suppression kinetics (Phase 2) is a model prediction, not something hardcoded in. Task structure (10 sessions × 150 trials × 16 animals/group, 80-20 contingency with randomized reversal blocks) matches the real cohort's session count and reward contingency; trial/session/cohort counts beyond that are reasonable assumed defaults, not the exact real numbers. Full derivation and every flagged assumption: sst_pv_model/params.py.

Replication / power study

A single simulated cohort can't establish how reliable an effect is. This runs the equivalent "experiment" 12 independent times, each at n=16/group — the top of the real 12-16/group range, not an inflated cohort — and combines evidence via Fisher's method, which sharpens significance honestly instead of pretending a 40+/group cohort could ever be collected.

Correction: the late-session claim does not replicate

An earlier single-run report noted, as an "unforced" bonus finding, that the model's young>old effect was weaker in early sessions and stronger in late sessions — superficially matching the real data's late-session-specific kappa decline. That does not hold up under replication. Across 12 independent runs, late sessions show the weakest and least consistent effect (mean d = +0.05, median slightly negative at −0.08, only 6/12 in the correct direction, combined p = 0.073, not significant) — if anything, early sessions are more robust, the opposite pattern from the real data. There is no session-number-dependent mechanism anywhere in this model, so there was never a principled reason to expect that correspondence. It was a coincidence of one random draw, not a real model property, and should not have been reported with the confidence it originally was.

Bottom line: treat this model as a sufficiency demonstration for the overall young > old perseveration direction — now reasonably well supported once properly powered across replications at a real cohort size — but not, currently, as an explanation for why the real effect is specifically late-session. That would require an explicit session/experience-dependent mechanism this model doesn't have.

Mechanism check

Verified directly, independent of the downstream behavioral fit: after an identical simulated "win" for one pool, followed by an identical inter-trial interval, the young-parameterized network retains measurably more residual winner/loser output asymmetry than the old-parameterized one (regression-tested in tests/test_network.py). No stickiness term exists anywhere in the circuit's equations — every bit of the behavioral kappa effect above is a downstream consequence of this differential reset.

Open assumptions

Everything below is flagged in code (sst_pv_model/params.py::ASSUMED_PARAMETERS, printed by every run) so results are never mistaken for a tighter claim than they support:

  1. SST/PV fold-changes are real but graph-read from dose-response curves, not exact per-cell table values.
  2. Interneuron time constants (τPV=15ms, τSST=100ms) are literature-typical, not measured in this dataset.
  3. Trial/session/cohort counts (150 trials/session, 16 animals/group) are reasonable defaults; only the 10-session count and 80-20 contingency are anchored to the real cohort.
  4. Recurrent coupling, input gain, decision noise, activation shape, and the network's internal value-learning rate are free/robustness parameters, swept in the sensitivity analysis above.
  5. No PV↔SST synaptic coupling, and no input-side (feedforward EPSC / intrinsic excitability) age-dependence — a deliberate simplification given two real, opposing electrical-stimulation findings (reduced feedforward EPSC onto PV cells, increased PV intrinsic excitability with age) that plausibly partially offset; revisit if matched quantitative magnitudes for both become available.

References

Full detail, quotes, and DOIs in REFERENCES.md. Condensed:

  • Wilson, Runyan, Wang & Sur (2012), Nature 488:343. PV divides (gain), SST subtracts (shifts) — the core functional assumption behind g_pv/g_sst.
  • Pfeffer, Xue, He, Huang & Scanziani (2013), Nat Neurosci 16:1068. SST→PV inhibition is real and asymmetric; PV→SST is comparatively weak — why the model omits PV↔SST coupling entirely rather than guessing a direction.
  • Wang (2002), Neuron 36:955. The canonical competing-pools/winner-take-all decision circuit this model's architecture is a simplified variant of.
  • Wilson & Cowan (1972), Biophysical Journal 12:1. The rate-model formalism used throughout.
  • eNeuro 9(1) (2022); Nat Commun 9:3576 (2018). PV synapses are faster/perisomatic, SST slower/dendritic — grounds the tau_pv < tau_sst kinetic asymmetry.

What is not literature-validated: the specific causal chain this test is built around — SST/PV kinetic and gain-type asymmetry → incomplete inter-trial reset → behavioral perseveration — is this project's own constructed hypothesis. Each individual piece above is independently grounded; the combination and its behavioral consequence is what Phase 1 tests, not a premise borrowed from prior work.
