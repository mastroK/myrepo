At a glance

Question: a rate-model decision circuit is given only the real, isolated SST/PV ChR2 output-synapse fold-changes across age (plus, as of the reopening above, the SST→PV coupling fold-change). Is that alone sufficient to produce higher behavioral perseveration (sticky Q-learning κ) in the young parameterization than the old one — with no stickiness term written into the circuit anywhere?

Answer: yes, directionally, and now on very firm statistical ground. Combined across 15 independent real-sized (16/group) simulated cohorts, young > old κ reaches significance in every session window (Fisher's combined p < 4×10-8 throughout) and is not fragile to any of the circuit's named free parameters (time constants, coupling strength).

One claim retracted: an earlier single-run report claimed the model also reproduced the real data's late-session-specific kappa decline. That does not replicate — see Replication study. Treat this as a sufficiency demonstration for the overall direction, not an explanation of the late-session timing.

Background

Isolated, cell-type-specific ChR2 slice-electrophysiology recordings show two age-related synaptic changes: SST→pyramidal output strength declines with age, while PV→pyramidal output strength increases. Feedforward IPSC kinetics in the compound (non-isolated) signal shift toward PV-like speed with age — a composite measurement kept deliberately out of Phase 1/2's parameterization, since it conflates several separate quantities (see References).

Separately, sticky Q-learning fits (Rescorla-Wagner value updates + a choice-stickiness parameter κ) to the real 80-20 bandit cohort show male mice becoming reliably less perseverative with age, an effect specific to late sessions (6-10 of 10) that survives correction for multiple comparisons.

Phase 1 asks whether a circuit built only from the first fact is sufficient to produce the qualitative direction of the second, with perseveration emerging from network dynamics rather than being fit or coded in.

Model construction

A Wilson-Cowan-style rate circuit: two excitatory pools (E0, E1) accumulate value/evidence for the bandit's two options. Each pool recruits its own local PV and SST interneuron drive, which inhibits the competing pool — standard lateral/cross-inhibition wiring for winner-take-all decision circuits.

PV
fast · divisive gain on the competing pool's output
SST
slow · subtractive shift of the competing pool's input

Neither the excitatory pools nor the interneuron populations are reset between trials or sessions—session boundaries excepted, where state resets along with the internal value estimate, matching real sessions being separated by rest days. Whatever choice-repetition bias emerges is a side effect of how completely PV (fast) vs. SST (slow) inhibition clears residual pool activity during the inter-trial interval, regression-tested directly (see Mechanism check) rather than assumed.

Originally the model had no direct PV↔SST synaptic connection in either direction — sidestepping a real, asymmetric connectivity question (SST inhibits PV; the reverse is comparatively weak) that didn't need resolving for the original test. As of the 2026-11-07 reopening (see callout above), the SST→PV arm is now modeled: SST's own slow, local drive discounts PV's cross-pool divisive reach (a fractional, bounded discount — an absolute-subtraction version was tried first and destabilized the circuit). PV→SST remains unmodeled. Full literature grounding for every design choice is in References.

Parameterization

SST and PV synaptic weights are set from real ChR2 dose-response curves (evoked charge, fC/pF, vs. laser power), read at the saturating (~15 mW) plateau of each age group's curve — graph-read estimates, not exact per-cell values.

Cell typeYoung (6-9wk)Old (16-36wk)Fold-change (old/young)
SST output charge~16,100 fC/pF~11,700 fC/pF0.73
PV output charge~3,650 fC/pF~5,150 fC/pF1.4

Interneuron kinetics (PV τ=15 ms, SST τ=100 ms) are literature-typical values, held fixed across age — only the output weights above scale with age, so any age-shift in emergent suppression kinetics (Phase 2) is a model prediction, not something hardcoded in. Task structure (10 sessions × 150 trials × 16 animals/group, 80-20 contingency with randomized reversal blocks) matches the real cohort's session count and reward contingency; trial/session/cohort counts beyond that are reasonable assumed defaults, not the exact real numbers. Full derivation and every flagged assumption: sst_pv_model/params.py.

Correction: the late-session claim does not replicate (still stands post-reopening)

An earlier single-run report noted, as an "unforced" bonus finding, that the model's young>old effect was weaker in early sessions and stronger in late sessions — superficially matching the real data's late-session-specific kappa decline. That does not hold up under replication, pre- or post-reopening. Pre-reopening, across 12 independent runs, late sessions showed the weakest and least consistent effect (mean d = +0.05, median slightly negative at −0.08, only 6/12 in the correct direction, combined p = 0.073, not significant). Post-reopening (15 replications, SST→PV coupling included), the qualitative pattern is unchanged: late sessions still have the lowest mean effect size (+0.57 vs. +0.95 all-sessions, +0.79 early) and by far the lowest individual-replication hit rate (5/15 = 33% individually significant, vs. 15/15 and 13/15 for the other two windows) — still the least consistent window on a per-replication basis, even though the much larger overall effect size now makes even this "weakest" window's pooled evidence overwhelming (Fisher's p = 1.44e-08). If anything, early/all sessions remain comparatively the more consistent windows — the opposite pattern from the real data's late-session-specific effect. There is no session-number-dependent mechanism anywhere in this model, so there was never a principled reason to expect that correspondence, and the reopening does not revive it. It was a coincidence of one random draw, not a real model property, and should not have been reported with the confidence it originally was.

Bottom line: treat this model as a sufficiency demonstration for the overall young > old perseveration direction — now on very firm statistical ground post-reopening — but not, currently, as an explanation for why the real effect is specifically late-session. That would require an explicit session/experience-dependent mechanism this model doesn't have.

Mechanism check

Revised 2026-11-07. Pre-reopening: after an identical simulated "win" for one pool, followed by an identical inter-trial interval, the young-parameterized network retained more residual winner/loser output asymmetry than the old-parameterized one, with old's own asymmetry staying positive (just smaller). That no longer holds at the confirmed SST→PV coupling values — in this isolated single-trial probe, old's residual asymmetry is now negative (the pool that just lost ends up with higher output than the pool that just won), not merely a weaker version of the young effect (regression-tested in tests/test_network.py, updated to assert this directly).

This isolated probe's reversal does not reflect the real behavioral result — see the Reopened callout at the top: the actual kappa effect got stronger once simulated with realistic trial-to-trial noise and value learning, not weaker or reversed. The isolated probe (a single forced extreme win, no noise, no learning, full ITI relaxation) is evidently not a reliable stand-in for the full multi-trial dynamics at these parameter values. Why, specifically, is an open question, not yet resolved. No stickiness term exists anywhere in the circuit's equations either way — every bit of the behavioral kappa effect above remains a downstream consequence of circuit dynamics, not something coded in directly.

Open assumptions

Everything below is flagged in code (sst_pv_model/params.py::ASSUMED_PARAMETERS, printed by every run) so results are never mistaken for a tighter claim than they support:

  1. SST/PV fold-changes are real but graph-read from dose-response curves, not exact per-cell table values.
  2. Interneuron time constants (τPV=15ms, τSST=100ms) are literature-typical, not measured in this dataset.
  3. Trial/session/cohort counts (150 trials/session, 16 animals/group) are reasonable defaults; only the 10-session count and 80-20 contingency are anchored to the real cohort.
  4. Recurrent coupling, input gain, decision noise, activation shape, and the network's internal value-learning rate are free/robustness parameters, swept in the sensitivity analysis above.
  5. SST→PV coupling now modeled (as of 2026-11-07), PV→SST still is not — the SST→PV gain values (0.25 young, 0.75 old) are confirmed point estimates but not graph-read with the same precision as the SST/PV output fold-changes; the fractional-discount formalization is a modeling choice, not a literal biophysical reconstruction (see sst_pv_model/network.py). PV→SST remains unmodeled per Pfeffer et al. 2013's connectivity asymmetry. No input-side (feedforward EPSC / intrinsic excitability) age-dependence is modeled — a deliberate simplification given two real, opposing electrical-stimulation findings (reduced feedforward EPSC onto PV cells, increased PV intrinsic excitability with age) that plausibly partially offset; revisit if matched quantitative magnitudes for both become available.

References

Full detail, quotes, and DOIs in REFERENCES.md. Condensed:

  • Wilson, Runyan, Wang & Sur (2012), Nature 488:343. PV divides (gain), SST subtracts (shifts) — the core functional assumption behind g_pv/g_sst.
  • Pfeffer, Xue, He, Huang & Scanziani (2013), Nat Neurosci 16:1068. SST→PV inhibition is real and asymmetric; PV→SST is comparatively weak — why the model now includes the SST→PV arm (as of the 2026-11-07 reopening, grounded in a new user-reported age-dependent IPSC kinetic change in that specific synapse) but still omits PV→SST rather than guessing its magnitude.
  • Wang (2002), Neuron 36:955. The canonical competing-pools/winner-take-all decision circuit this model's architecture is a simplified variant of.
  • Wilson & Cowan (1972), Biophysical Journal 12:1. The rate-model formalism used throughout.
  • eNeuro 9(1) (2022); Nat Commun 9:3576 (2018). PV synapses are faster/perisomatic, SST slower/dendritic — grounds the tau_pv < tau_sst kinetic asymmetry.

What is not literature-validated: the specific causal chain this test is built around — SST/PV kinetic and gain-type asymmetry → incomplete inter-trial reset → behavioral perseveration — is this project's own constructed hypothesis. Each individual piece above is independently grounded; the combination and its behavioral consequence is what Phase 1 tests, not a premise borrowed from prior work.
